## Decay... of intent

## So, what do we keep in high fidelity... and what do we compress with the hope of not needing in the future?

## "Write it down"... public ledgers. immutable states of uncompressed detail... but we can only "know" so much without needing to compress *something*.

## We have left cave paintings as compressed references of intent. A thing we can point to on a cave wall, and in the mind of the person who painted it, the scene of greatness is re-lived as if it were happening in the *now*. The "compressed intent" of the caveman became the ability to *decompress* it later in the stories telling. "Remember when we took down that freaking *bear* last season? Cha homies... we *did* that." Over time, perhaps the stories got less real, and more interpretations crept in. The painting was... maybe higher resolution for longer, but still has a decay. Eventually, all we can do is say "there was some kind of intelegence here, and it had a thought about a bear one day."

## Ribbons and Medals on the chests of decorated war heros. "I was there when". They can point to the token, and decompress an entire *story* in high resolution. And even if you find someone else with the same decoration, their lived experience of the same event will be entirely different. And from just pointing to a simple signet that represents the event.

## Writing stuff down... into pictographs and formal rules so others can "decompress" the intent in their own head, with fidelity that *they* bring to the story. Removed from the original authors direct intent, but through lense of interpretation from an outside agent.
